WoW_NavigationData
==================

Walking-navigation map data for WoW Forever, made for addons that guide blind players.

This repository holds the data only: the physical waypoint network of the game world, the catalogues
of things a player may want to walk to, and the transit points that join the maps together. A small
demo addon shows how an addon can read the data and turn it into spoken and audible route guidance.

1. WHAT THIS REPOSITORY IS
--------------------------

The goal is on-foot navigation for blind players. An addon that reads this data can answer
"how do I walk from here to the innkeeper?" with a short list of waypoints, each one a place the
player can reach by walking straight from the previous one.

Every zone comes with three kinds of data:

  * The physical network (navdata.lua). A sparse set of waypoints, the straight links between them,
    and a grid of "attachment cells" that lets any position in the zone be tied to the network.
    Every link was verified offline against the walkable geometry of the game, so an addon does
    not have to invent a path of its own.

  * Logical targets (logical_targets.lua). The catalogue of places a player may want to reach:
    NPCs (quest givers, vendors, trainers, flight masters, innkeepers, ...), creatures (mobs),
    and game objects (chests, herbs and ore veins, mailboxes, ...). A target carries a position and a
    name, never a waypoint number; the addon attaches it to the network when it needs it.

  * Dynamic targets (dyn_targets.lua). Targets that are about getting somewhere else: dungeon
    entrances, portals, elevators, boat and zeppelin docks, the stops of the Deeprun Tram, the dive
    points of the Ruins of Blackfathom, the portals and the Wind Bridge of Zephras Isle, and the
    crossings between neighbouring zones.

Routes are always on foot. Walking into a portal or into a trigger such as the entrance of the Deeprun
Tram, riding an elevator, clicking a portal on Zephras Isle and waiting for and stepping into its Wind
Bridge count as walking. Boarding a boat, a zeppelin, a skyboat or the tram is
not part of a route; their docks and waiting points are targets, so a player can be guided to the dock
and takes the vehicle personally. Flight paths are not modelled at all.

At a glance (snapshot of the data in this repository):

  51 zones in 4 continent groups   EasternKingdoms 26, Kalimdor 23, DeeprunTram 1, ZephrasIsle 1
  about 0.86 million waypoints     about 2.25 million links, about 0.92 million attachment cells
  about 111,300 logical targets    242 dynamic targets
  3,318 seams between zones        63 neighbouring zone pairs
  about 54 MB of Lua files         the largest zone file (The Barrens, navdata.lua) is about 2.6 MB;
                                   the logical target catalogues of all zones take about 7 MB of it

Supported game version
  WoW Forever (client series 1.60.1; the demo addon declares interface 16001).

Not supported
  Other game versions and expansions. Instances: the only instance map in the data is the Deeprun
  Tram. There is no dungeon, raid or battleground interior. Dungeon entrances exist as targets, the
  insides do not exist. The Ruins of Gilneas are not included (the client does not ship terrain for
  them).

The files are generated by a build process that is not part of this repository. Do not edit them by
hand (see section 13).

    ZephrasIsle/          index.lua, and one zone folder (Zephras Isle)

    readme.txt            how to install the demo
    WowVisionNavigation/  the reference addon that consumes the data

Every zone folder, for example Forever/Kalimdor/Teldrassil/, holds the same three files:

  navdata.lua           the waypoint network
  logical_targets.lua   NPCs, creatures and objects
  dyn_targets.lua       transit facilities and zone transitions

A "continent" here is one folder with one index.lua. EasternKingdoms is native map 0 and Kalimdor is
native map 1. DeeprunTram (native map 369) and ZephrasIsle (native map 2991) have maps of their own
and are therefore continents with a single zone each.

Zone folders are named after the English zone name without spaces or punctuation (TheBarrens,
UnGoroCrater, Shendralas). The name is not always identical to the display name: Stormwind City is
the folder Stormwind, Elwynn Forest is Elwynn. The authoritative link between folder and zone is the
field "dir" in the continent index. All three zone files are always present; a zone without transit
points has a dyn_targets.lua with an empty target list.

3. COORDINATES, UNITS AND IDENTIFIERS
-------------------------------------

Units
  All lengths are in yards. All coordinates are world coordinates of the game client. There is no
  zone-local or map-relative coordinate system anywhere in the files.

Axes
  x grows to the north, y grows to the west, z is the height. Every coordinate is a triple (x, y, z).
  Positions are only meaningful together with the native map ID of the file they come from: 0 for
  Eastern Kingdoms, 1 for Kalimdor, 369 for the Deeprun Tram and 2991 for Zephras Isle.

Precision
  Waypoint coordinates are stored with a resolution of 0.1 yard (as integers, see section 6).
  Targets carry up to three decimals.

Normalised map positions
  The game's world map reports positions as fractions (nx, ny) from 0 to 1, with nx growing from left
  to right and ny from top to bottom. The index gives the world rectangle of each map ("worldBoxes",
  section 5), and the conversion is:

      x = xmax - ny * (xmax - xmin)        (map top to bottom runs along the north axis)
      y = ymax - nx * (ymax - ymin)        (map left to right runs along the west axis)

Identifiers
  mapId / nativeMapId   Native map of the world: 0, 1, 369 or 2991.
  zoneId                One zone of the data set. It is the UiMap ID of the zone's map (for example
                        84 for Stormwind City, 1416 for Alterac Mountains) and always one of the
                        keys of the zone's "uiMaps" in the index. The files are keyed by it.
  uiMaps                All UiMap IDs that belong to a zone (the zone map itself and, for some zones,
                        further maps). Each has its own world box.
  nameAreaId            Where present, the AreaTable ID of a place; the client turns it into a
                        localised place name (section 8).
  entry                 Numeric template ID of a creature or object in the source data.
  waypoint ID           Index of the waypoint inside its zone file, counted from 0 in file order
                        ("local ID"). The global ID that the index and the actions use is
                        zoneId * 10,000,000 + local ID. Example: waypoint 40 of Tirisfal Glades
                        (zone 18) is 180000040; waypoint 18748 of Silverpine Forest (zone 1421) is
                        14210018748. A route searcher typically also creates temporary "virtual"
                        points (start, target, points on a link); the demo addon numbers them from
                        10^12 upward. They are never stored in a file.
  seam ID               Zone ID of the zone owning the GATEWAY end of the seam * 1000 + a running
                        number, for example 18001 or 1421002 (section 5).

Waypoint and seam IDs are stable only for one export of a zone. When a zone is regenerated, its
waypoint numbers change. Never save them (for example in saved variables) across data updates; save
coordinates instead.

All Lua files are UTF-8.


4. HOW THE FILES ARE LOADED
---------------------------

Every .lua file is plain Lua that only assigns global tables. Besides the assignments there is no
code except the table.concat calls that join long strings. There is no external dependency and no
compression format. The files are meant to be listed in an addon's .toc file and to be loaded all at
once when the game starts; the demo addon does this (its .toc lists every file under maps/). The
zone data stays compact (long strings and number lists) until a zone is decoded, which a consumer
should do on demand (section 10).

  maps/<Continent>/index.lua                   WVN7Index["<Continent>"] = { mapId, zones }
  maps/<Continent>/<Zone>/navdata.lua          WVN7[zoneId]        = W  (the raw tables of section 6)
  maps/<Continent>/<Zone>/logical_targets.lua  WVN7Logical[zoneId] = { ..., classes = { ... }, targets = { ... } }
                                               (compact format, section 7)
  maps/<Continent>/<Zone>/dyn_targets.lua      WVN7Dyn[zoneId]     = { ..., targets = { ... } }

Each file creates its global table when it does not exist yet (for example "WVN7 = WVN7 or {}"), so
the load order of the files does not matter. A zone can be loaded without its neighbours, but a route
that crosses a zone border needs the seams of the index and the navdata of both zones.


5. index.lua - THE CONTINENT INDEX AND THE SEAMS
------------------------------------------------

The index of a continent lists its zones and holds the table of seams, the connections between
neighbouring zones. A shortened entry (Tirisfal Glades):

  WVN7Index["EasternKingdoms"] = {mapId=0,zones={
  [18]={name="Tirisfal Glades",dir="TirisfalGlades",zoneId=18,base=180000000,nativeMapId=0,
    instance=false,uiMaps={[1420]=true,[18]=true,[465]=true},
    worldBoxes={[1420]={xmin=825.0,xmax=3837.5,ymin=-1485.42,ymax=3033.33}, ...},
    files={"navdata.lua","logical_targets.lua","dyn_targets.lua"},
    fingerprint="9894897A...",
    seams={{a=180000040,bz=1421,bl=45301,bt=204,cost=2.5,id=18001,bref="link",
            bp={1418.5,493.5,34.7}}, ...}}, ...}}

Fields of a zone entry
  name, dir, zoneId   English display name, folder name, and the zone ID (the key of the entry).
  base                zoneId * 10,000,000, the offset of the global waypoint IDs of the zone.
  nativeMapId         The native map the zone lives on. Equal to the mapId of the continent.
  instance            true for the Deeprun Tram (a map of its own entered by a trigger), false for
                      all open-world zones, including Zephras Isle.
  uiMaps              The set of UiMap IDs of the zone.
  worldBoxes          For each UiMap ID the rectangle {xmin,xmax,ymin,ymax} in world coordinates
                      that the map covers. Used to turn the map position of the player into world
                      coordinates (section 3) and to find the candidate zones of a point. Boxes of
                      neighbouring zones overlap, and boxes of different native maps overlap too
                      (continent and island), so restrict candidates to the native map you are on.
  nativeWorldBox      Only the Deeprun Tram: the box {xmin,xmax,ymin,ymax} of the network of the
                      instance plus 30 yards. Use it as a plausibility check on player positions;
                      the maps listed in uiMaps cover only part of the tunnel.
  files               The three files of the zone.
  fingerprint         64 hex characters identifying this export. The same value stands in the
                      header of the zone's navdata.lua, which lets a consumer verify that an index
                      and a zone file belong together (they match for all 51 zones here).
  seams               The seam records that this zone's entry holds (see below). Many entries
                      hold none.

What a seam is
  A seam is a short connection between two waypoint positions on the two sides of a zone border.
  Walking across the border means walking along the seam. A seam record has these fields:

  id        The seam ID: 1000 * (zone ID of the zone that owns the GATEWAY end) + a running number.
            All IDs are unique in the data set.
  cost      Length in yards of the checked connection (median about 1.3 yd on land and about 6 yd
            across water).
  water     true when the seam is a swimming connection. Treat it with the swimming cost factor.
  a, b      Global waypoint ID of that end, when the end is a waypoint.
  az,al,at  When end a lies on a link instead of at a waypoint: the zone ID, the link number (counted
            from 0 in the CSR order of section 6) and the position t (1 to 255; the end lies
            t/256 of the way from the link's start waypoint to its end waypoint).
  bz,bl,bt  The same for end b.
  aref      Present when end a is an existing part of the network and not a GATEWAY waypoint:
            "node" or "link". bref is the same for end b.
  ap, bp    The position {x,y,z} of the end. It is given for every end that carries aref or bref.

  Exactly one end of every seam is a dedicated waypoint with the GATEWAY code (it has neither aref
  nor ap). It lists the seam ID in its "seams" extra (section 6). The other end is either an
  ordinary waypoint of the neighbouring zone ("node") or a point on one of its links ("link").

Seams are one-sided in the data
  Every border crossing is stored exactly once. The record sits in the "seams" list of the zone with
  the smaller zone ID of the pair, with that zone as end a. Which of the two ends is the GATEWAY
  waypoint depends on the build: it belongs to the zone that was generated after its neighbour. The
  neighbour, generated earlier, was left untouched, which is why its end refers to what already
  existed (a waypoint or a point on a link).

Seams are symmetric in use
  A seam can be walked in both directions. The demo addon, for example, enters the seam from
  whichever of its two ends the route reaches first. To find every seam of a zone, a consumer
  therefore has to look at the seam lists of all zones of the continent, not just at the list of the
  zone itself. Example: the 29 seams between Elwynn Forest (37) and Stormwind City (84) are all
  kept in the entry of zone 37; the entry of zone 84 holds none.

A worked example (seam 18001, Tirisfal Glades -> Silverpine Forest):
  End a is the GATEWAY waypoint 180000040 = waypoint 40 of zone 18, at (1420.2, 495.0, 35.7).
  End b is a point on link 45301 of zone 1421, at t = 204 of 256 between the link's two waypoints
  (17748 and 17751 there), at (1418.5, 493.5, 34.7). The connection is 2.5 yd long.
  In navdata.lua of Tirisfal Glades, waypoint 40 carries the GATEWAY code, and its extra reads
  seams={31,18004,9,18001,...}: the pairs are (waypoint delta, seam ID), so 31 + 9 = 40 carries
  seam 18001 (section 6). A router that wants to cross creates a temporary waypoint on link 45301 at
  t/256, connects it to waypoint 180000040 with cost 2.5, and also connects it to both ends of the
  link (subject to the link's direction).

How a consumer should treat seams
  The index is data to check, not to trust blindly. The demo addon verifies that the GATEWAY end has
  the GATEWAY code and lists the seam ID, that a node end lies within 0.06 yd of the stated position,
  and that a link end exists, has t in 1 to 255 and lies at the stated position. A seam that fails any
  check is ignored ("fail closed"). In the data of this repository, all 3,318 seams (6,636 seam
  ends) pass the checks for existing waypoints, links and seam IDs.


6. navdata.lua - THE WAYPOINT NETWORK
-------------------------------------

What the file contains
  * Waypoints with a position, an area type, a set of codes (why the waypoint exists), and an
    arrival radius. Each waypoint is a possible steering target for the player.
  * Links between waypoints. A link is a straight line, was verified offline as walkable, and has a
    flag set and a direction.
  * Attachment cells, a world-aligned grid per zone, which tie any position to the network.
  * Extras: gate widths, branch points, seam IDs, capture records of transit facilities, and the
    actions (transit links).
  Link costs are not stored. The length of a link is computed from the coordinates when the zone is
  decoded.

Waypoint area types
  OPEN   Open terrain: wide, free land that is not otherwise special. Links tend to be long.
  FINE   Everything else that can be walked: buildings, bridges, stairs, caves, jetties, tree
         houses, narrow passages. Links are short, and the route is precise.
  WATER  Water that is at least 1.5 yd deep, where the player swims.

Waypoint codes (bits; one waypoint can carry several)
    1 CORNER    A bend of a route.
    2 GATE      A door, bridge head, cave mouth, or foot of a staircase: a place where open and
                fine terrain meet. Every GATE waypoint has a stored half width.
    4 ROAD      Junction, end, or bend of a road.
    8 EXIT      A place to leave the water, with a directed exit link.
   16 LAYER     A stairs foot or head, end of a ramp, change of floor.
   32 ACTION    Transit, portal, boarding, trigger. Always comes with an extra record.
   64 GATEWAY   One end of a seam to a neighbouring zone.
  128 TARGET    The access point of a target; always the end of a short branch.
  256 COVERAGE  A waypoint that only exists to cover open areas.
  512 BRANCH    The point where a branch attaches in the middle of a link; the link is split there.

Link flags (bits)
    1  road       The link runs on a road.
    2  water exit The link leads from water to land.
    4  fine       A link in FINE terrain.
    8  swimming   A link through water.
  The length of a link and its flags decide its cost; see section 10. Every link also has a direction:
  0 both ways, 1 only from its first to its second waypoint, 2 only the opposite way. (Almost all
  links, more than 99.8 percent, are two-way.)

File structure
  A navdata.lua file consists of a header, a registration line, and one block per field.

    -- WVN7 schema 7, zone DeeprunTram (id 499); written by ...
    local W = {version=7,zoneId=499,mapId=369,prototype=0,nodeCount=965,linkCount=1404,
      cellCount=4245,fingerprint="611A0ADB...",codes={
    1025,5376,5121,1280,1,5248,1152,5408,5632,4097,256,1026,1312,4608,5153,5280,5377,8320,8448
    },radii={
    5,10,20
    }}
    WVN7 = WVN7 or {}
    WVN7[499] = W
    -- WVN7 factions: hostile outposts as waypoint ranges, per faction {gap, length - 1, ...}; ...
    W.factions = {alliance={0,964}}
    ;(function() W.x = {
    -215,216,-136,-8,-243,-22,1,27,115,107,9,-36,...
    } end)()
    ;(function() W.y = { ... } end)()
    ...

  Each field sits in its own function, so that no single Lua function exceeds the limit on the number
  of constants. Number lists are written as {a,b,c,...}; character fields are written either as one
  string or as table.concat({"...","...",...}) of string pieces of at most 60,000 characters.

  The header values:
    version       The format version. Always 7.
    zoneId        The zone ID.
    mapId         The native map of the zone.
    prototype     Always 0.
    nodeCount     Number of waypoints. linkCount: number of links. cellCount: number of cells.
                  A decoder uses them to validate the field lengths.
    fingerprint   See section 5.
    codes         The code dictionary of this zone (see below), at most 92 entries.
    radii         The arrival radii used in the zone in units of 0.1 yd (5,10,20 mean 0.5, 1.0, 2.0
                  yd).

Faction outposts (W.factions)
  Right after the registration line a zone may carry one extra statement, W.factions. It marks the
  waypoints that belong to an outpost or capital city of one faction, so that a router can keep an
  Alliance player out of Horde towns and the other way round:
    horde      the waypoints of Horde outposts and cities (hostile for Alliance players)
    alliance   the waypoints of Alliance outposts and cities (hostile for Horde players)
  Each list holds waypoint number ranges as pairs {gap, length - 1, gap, length - 1, ...}. Waypoint
  numbers are 0-based, the ranges ascend: the first range starts at its gap, every further range
  starts at (end of the previous range + 1 + gap) and covers length waypoints. Example:
  horde={12,3,40,7} marks the waypoints 12 to 15 and 56 to 63. Waypoints in no range are neutral
  (Booty Bay, Ratchet, Gadgetzan and the open land). A zone without W.factions is neutral, and a
  capital city is one range over all its waypoints (the Deeprun Tram counts as Alliance).
  The statement is added after the export and is not part of the fingerprint: waypoints, links,
  cells and seams are the same with and without it, so a consumer that does not know W.factions
  can ignore it. The outposts come from the client's flight masters and from several wiki and
  WoW Forever sources (a town counts when the sources agree); the extent of an outpost is its sub-area in the client's
  area table (the terrain chunks that carry it).
  How the demo addon uses it: it routes around the hostile waypoints first (a detour). Only when no
  route without them exists, the route goes through and the player is warned; a route never fails
  because of an outpost. An outpost in which the start or the target lies is open, so a player can
  leave a town they stand in and reach a target inside a hostile town (with a warning).
  /vn outposts on|off (German: /vn posten an|aus) switches the avoidance; the setting is saved.

Encoding
  Character fields use an alphabet of 92 printable ASCII characters: 0x21 to 0x7E without the double
  quote (0x22) and the backslash (0x5C). The value of a character is its position in that alphabet:

      ! = 0, # = 1, $ = 2, % = 3, & = 4, ' = 5, ( = 6, ...

  A number that needs more than one character is written as base-92 digits, most significant first.

  Waypoints, in file order (the order is spatially coherent, which keeps the deltas small, and has
  no other meaning). Waypoint i is the i-th element of every per-waypoint field.
    x, y, z   Position as integers in 0.1 yd. Each value is the difference to the previous waypoint
              (the first one is absolute), so the position is the running sum divided by 10.
    c         One character per waypoint: an index into the code dictionary "codes".
    s         One character per waypoint: the layer index. It is 0 everywhere in the shipped files.
    lo        One character per waypoint: the number of links that START at this waypoint.
    lt, lf    Per link (see below).
    e         Extras, sparse (see below).

  The code dictionary. A dictionary entry is a number made of four parts:

      value = bits + 1024 * area + 4096 * radiusClass + 1048576 * noExit

    bits         The sum of the waypoint code bits above.
    area         0 OPEN, 1 FINE, 2 WATER.
    radiusClass  An index into "radii". The result is the arrival radius of the waypoint: the
                 distance at which the player counts as having reached it.
    noExit       1 on a water waypoint whose body of water has no verified exit. A router should
                 not offer such a waypoint as an alternative start.

  Links (compressed sparse rows). Links are stored once per pair, from the waypoint with the lower
  index u to the one with the higher index v (u < v). They are ordered by u. The number of the link
  is its position in the lt list, counted from 0.
    lo        How many links start at each waypoint. Reading lt from the top, the first lo[0]
              links start at waypoint 0, the next lo[1] at waypoint 1, and so on.
    lt        For each link: v - u (a positive number). So the link starts at u and ends at u + lt.
    lf        One character per link: direction * 16 + flags (direction 0/1/2, flags as above).

  Cells (the attachment grid), in a second list of fields indexed by cell number:
    cx, cy    The origin of the cell in units of 8 yd, again as differences to the previous cell.
              The origin is the corner with the smallest x and y; multiply by 8 to get yards.
    cz, ch    The vertical band of the cell: cz is the middle in 0.1 yd (difference to the previous
              cell), ch the half height of the band in 0.1 yd.
    cc        The cell class, one character: 0 = OPEN (16 yd square), 1 = FINE (8 yd square),
              2 = WATER (32 yd square).
    cl        The layer of the cell, one character. Always 0 in the shipped files.
    an        One character per cell: how many attachments the cell has (up to 6 in the shipped
              files).
    ar        One number per attachment: a running sum of differences, never reset between cells.
              A value of 0 or more is a waypoint (its local ID). A negative value v encodes a point
              on a link: with n = -v, the link is floor(n / 256) and the point lies at t = n mod 256
              of 256 along the link, from its start waypoint u to its end waypoint v.
    aq        Three characters per attachment: a 16-bit mask as three base-92 digits.
              The cell is divided into a 4 x 4 raster of fields; bit 4*fy + fx belongs to the field
              with fx = floor((x - cellX) / (size / 4)) and fy = floor((y - cellY) / (size / 4)).
              A set bit says that from a position in that field the attachment can be reached
              directly (this was verified offline).

Extras (field "e")
  A table with these optional keys:
    gates      The half width of the passage at a GATE waypoint. Pairs {idDelta, halfWidth}: the
               waypoint ID as a running sum of deltas, the half width in 0.1 yd.
    branches   For a BRANCH waypoint, the two neighbours that belong to the two halves of the same
               split link. Triples {idDelta, neighbour1 - id, neighbour2 - id}. A route that
               just walks straight through both halves does not need to turn there.
    seams      The seams that end at a GATEWAY waypoint. Pairs {idDelta, seamId}; a waypoint with
               several seams repeats with delta 0. The seam itself is in the index.
    captures   A list of records for the transit facilities of the zone, as explained in section 8.
    actions    A list of directed transit links (see below).

  Actions (field e.actions). Each action is a directed link from a waypoint of this zone to a waypoint
  of the same or another zone:
    id, kind       An identifier, and the kind: "tram", "lift" (elevator), "portal", "dive" or
                   "surface" (the two directions of the dive transition), or "wind_bridge" (the
                   Wind Bridge of Zephras Isle). The 56 actions of the data are 38 lifts, 8 tram
                   actions, 7 portals, the dive and the surface action, and the Wind Bridge.
    from           Local waypoint ID of the start (an ACTION waypoint).
    to, toZone, toMapId   Global waypoint ID of the destination, its zone and its native map.
    cost           The cost of the action in yards; a search uses it like the length of a link.
    activation     {mode, proof, verified}. mode is "walk_into_volume" (the player triggers the
                   action by walking into a volume: most portals and the tram entrances),
                   "board" (the player boards a moving platform: elevators and the tram),
                   "click" (the player interacts with a game object, a portal that is no trigger:
                   the three portals of Zephras Isle; the demo addon finds the object by WowVision
                   soft-targeting), "wait_for_signal" (the player waits at the start until the
                   bridge sound, then steps into the wind current: the Wind Bridge), or
                   "dive" / "surface" (the dive transition).
    arrival        How the arrival is recognised, e.g. "transport_stop_volume",
                   "native_map_volume", "same_map_distinct_volume".
    sourceVolume, arrivalVolume   Volume where the action starts / where the traveller arrives:
                   a sphere {radius,x,y,z} or an oriented box
                   {half_x,half_y,half_z,yaw_radians,x,y,z}.
    conditions     {status="unconditional", proof=...}, with a "decision" note on the project
                   transitions of Zephras Isle. All actions in the data are unconditional.
    timing         {timeout_seconds}: the time limit of the action. For rides it is a full cycle of
                   the vehicle, for all other actions 30 seconds. The Wind Bridge has
                   {no_time_limit=true} instead: the wait for the signal is not limited.
    faction        Present on the two arrival variants of the Valanaar portal: "alliance" or
                   "horde". An action with a faction is meant for the players of that faction
                   only; the demo core uses it only for a player of that faction (and not at all
                   when the faction of the player is unknown). An action without it is for
                   everybody.
    approximate    true on the four actions of Zephras Isle: their positions are estimates, and the
                   demo addon tells the player so.

  Capture records (e.captures). One record for every transit facility that is tied to an ACTION
  waypoint (all facilities except the dungeon entrances), describing where and how the player
  stands: "waiting" records for docks and elevator platforms (x, y, z, waitingRadius of at
  most 2 yd, interactionMode "manual_wait", a boardingHintStatus, and for certified docks the
  orientation boardingDirX/boardingDirY and boardingPreviewDistance), and "entrance" or "exit"
  records for walk-in triggers (activationMode "walk_into_trigger", the point to walk from,
  activationPreX/Y/Z, and activationCaptureRadius). The records of the zone repeat the facility data
  of the matching dyn_targets.lua entries (see section 8).

A worked example: decoding the first waypoints and the first cell of the Deeprun Tram zone

  Waypoint 0 to 2. The first three numbers of W.x are -215, 216 and -136, those of W.y are 2, -246
  and -36, and those of W.z are -42, -96 and 96.
      waypoint 0 = (-21.5,   0.2,  -4.2)       -215 / 10, 2 / 10, -42 / 10
      waypoint 1 = (  0.1, -24.4, -13.8)       (-215+216) / 10, (2-246) / 10, (-42-96) / 10
      waypoint 2 = (-13.5, -28.0,  -4.2)       (-215+216-136) / 10, (2-246-36) / 10, (-42-96+96) / 10

  W.c begins "&#!...". '&' is 4, '#' is 1 and '!' is 0. The dictionary of this zone starts 1025,5376,
  5121,1280,1,..., so
      waypoint 0 has code    1             -> CORNER, area 0 (OPEN), radius class 0 -> 0.5 yd
      waypoint 1 has code 5376 = 256 + 1024 + 4096
                                           -> COVERAGE, area 1 (FINE), radius class 1 -> 1.0 yd
      waypoint 2 has code 1025 = 1 + 1024  -> CORNER, area 1 (FINE), radius class 0 -> 0.5 yd

  W.lo begins "'#": waypoint 0 has 5 links, waypoint 1 has 1. W.lt begins 3,9,18,29,30,42:
      link 0: waypoint 0 -> waypoint 3         link 1: waypoint 0 -> waypoint 9
      link 2: waypoint 0 -> waypoint 18        link 3: waypoint 0 -> waypoint 29
      link 4: waypoint 0 -> waypoint 30        link 5: waypoint 1 -> waypoint 43
  W.lf begins "!!!!!&": '!' = 0 is both ways without flags for links 0 to 4; '&' = 4 is direction 0
  (both ways) with flag 4 (fine) for link 5.

  The first cell: cx = -2, cy = -5, cz = -40, ch = 1, cc = '#' (1), an = '#' (1).
      origin (-16, -40) yd, middle height -4.0 yd, half height 0.1 yd, class 1 = FINE (8 yd square),
      one attachment.
  The first value of W.ar is -3109: n = 3109 = 12 * 256 + 37, so the attachment is the point t = 37 of
  256 along link 12. The first three characters of W.aq are ")g2": (7 * 92 + 68) * 92 + 16 = 65520
  = 0xFFF0, so the attachment serves fields 4 to 15 of the 4 x 4 raster of the cell.


7. logical_targets.lua - NPCS, MOBS AND OBJECTS
-----------------------------------------------

The file defines a catalogue of the targets of one zone. It comes in a compact form (format 2) that
keeps the constants of the file once in the header and what targets of one kind share once per
"class", so that a target itself is one short line. The catalogues of all zones together take about
7 MB for about 111,300 targets.

  -- Logical target catalogue, compact format 2. No graph bindings, no provenance.
  local LogicalTargets = {format=2,schema=1,layer="logical",zoneId=84,coordinateSpace="world",
     nativeMapId=0,availability="static",sourceSha256="...",converterSha256="...",
     payloadSha256="..."}
  LogicalTargets.classes = {
    {"npc",{"quest_giver","trainer"},376,"High Priestess Laurena (Priest Trainer)",
       "Hohepriesterin Laurena (Priesterlehrerin)"},
    {"npc","quest_giver",656,"Wilder Thistlenettle"},
    ...
    {"creature","npc_or_mob",1756,"Stormwind Royal Guard","Königswache von Stormwind",
       a="possible_spawn"},
    {"gameobject","object",2106,"Cathedral Square"},
    ...
    {"npc","quest_giver",11828,"Kelly Grant",a="possible_spawn"},
    {"mob","npc_or_mob",165189,"Cat"},
    {"herb","herb",1618,"Peacebloom","Friedensblume"},
    ...
  }
  LogicalTargets.targets = {
    {"creature_1079",1,-8512.4,862.364,109.927},
    {"creature_2435",2,-8468.64,501.28,99.912},
    ...
    {"creature_90482-11828",247,-8747,1065.82,90.062},
    {"questie_creature_165189_1519_0",438,-8646.59,1343.62,l={-55.407,0.2},p=true},
    ...
  }
  WVN7Logical = WVN7Logical or {}
  WVN7Logical[84] = LogicalTargets

Header fields
  format            2: the compact form described here, the only form of logical catalogues. Earlier
                    snapshots of this data also contained logical catalogues without this field (one
                    table per target with all fields in every row); there are none any more, and a
                    consumer should refuse a file with another format number (the demo addon does,
                    with a message).
  schema            The version of the target contract. Currently 1.
  layer             "logical" (the catalogue of this file; the dyn catalogue says "dyn").
  zoneId            The zone ID.
  coordinateSpace   "world": positions are in world coordinates (section 3). It holds for every target
                    of the file.
  nativeMapId       The native map of the zone (0, 1, 369 or 2991). It holds for every target.
  availability      The default availability of a class ("static"), see below.
  converterSha256, payloadSha256, sourceSha256
                    Hashes that identify the export. payloadSha256 is the SHA-256 of the canonical
                    JSON of the header constants and of the rows as expanded below.

classes: what the targets of one population share
  A class is a list; the position says what a value is.
    [1] kind          "npc", "mob", "creature", "gameobject", "herb", "mining" or "mailbox" (the
                      kinds in the shipped files; a consumer should also accept "search_area" and
                      "live", which do not occur). npc, mob and creature are spawns of a creature
                      (an NPC, a mob, a creature without a known role); gameobject, herb, mining
                      and mailbox are spawns of a game object (herb: a herb node, mining: an ore
                      vein). Two targets with the same kind group and the same entry are the same
                      population. In the shipped files, the 111,254 targets are 40,022 mob, 18,391
                      creature, 9,085 npc, 11,988 gameobject, 17,698 herb, 14,014 mining and 56
                      mailbox targets.
    [2] category      One English category key, or a list of keys. The ones in use: quest_giver,
                      trainer, vendor, flight_master, innkeeper, stable_master, auctioneer, banker,
                      spirit_healer, npc_or_mob, chest, herb, ore, object, quest_item,
                      interaction_object, mailbox, anvil, forge, and a few generic ones of the
                      form object_type_N (herb is the category of the kind "herb", ore that of the
                      kind "mining"). Localise them in the consumer. mailbox, anvil and forge
                      are recognised from the type of the game object in the source database (a
                      mailbox; a spell focus of the kind anvil or forge), never from the name.
    [3] entry         The template ID of the creature or object, or false when there is none (every
                      class of the shipped files has one).
                      Several targets with the same entry are different spawns of the same kind
                      (for example all the boars of a zone); a list that shows names should usually
                      fold them into one line.
    [4] nameEn        The English name.
    [5] nameDe        The German name; absent when it is the same as nameEn (the name is the same
                      in both languages, or no German name is known: about 8 percent of the
                      targets, 23 percent of the classes).
    a                 The availability when it is not the default of the header: "possible_spawn"
                      for a placement that need not be occupied at any moment (random or pooled
                      spawns, a single unconfirmed observation). "static" is the default.
    s                 A list of alternative names for searching; absent when there are none (no
                      class of the shipped files has one).

targets: one line per target
  [1] id            Unique ID within the zone, e.g. "creature_1079", "gameobject_18807" or
                    "questie_creature_251787_36_0" (the number in the first form is the ID of the
                    single spawn in the source data).
  [2] class         The index (starting at 1) into classes.
  [3] x, [4] y      World position in yards.
  [5] z             The height. If the source has no height and the network shows one walkable
                    level at the spot, z is that level's height. If it is not known, z is absent
                    (never 0; 0 is a height). A consumer treats "no z" as height unknown.
  l                 Present instead of z when a target without a source height lies over several
                    walkable floors: a list of the floor heights, lowest first. The consumer has to
                    find out which floor is meant (the demo addon asks the player).
  r                 The radius in yards of an area target (kind "search_area"; it does not occur in
                    the shipped files).
  p                 true for an approximate target: its position was attached to the network
                    afterwards (see "Attaching a target to the network").

A target carries no waypoint ID and no distance to the network. The data tells where the target
is; where the player enters the network to reach it is decided when the addon binds it (section 10).

Populations and the reachability rule
  A group of spawns of the same kind counts as reachable when at least one of its spawns can be
  reached. Spawns that the network could not reach were removed from the catalogue. Every target in
  the file was therefore reachable through the network when the zone was built (the last stretch
  may still be approximate, see below).

Attaching a target to the network
  Targets are not waypoints. A consumer finds the attachment cell above the target's position whose
  height band fits the target's z (with a tolerance), takes one of its attachments (waypoint or link
  point) as the destination, and routes to it. A WATER cell holds the height of the surface and
  everything below it is under water, so for a water cell only the upper bound of the band (plus the
  tolerance) counts: a position on the lake floor binds to the water cell above it. If no cell
  contains the position, the nearest
  cell within a radius is taken, and the target is reported as an approximate position (the demo
  addon says "approximate" and the distance). The demo addon uses these defaults: a search radius of
  30 yd around a target, a height tolerance of 3 yd (6 yd as a second attempt), and 3 yd around the
  position of the start.


8. dyn_targets.lua - TRANSIT AND ZONE TRANSITIONS
-------------------------------------------------

The dyn catalogue is a list of rows (a header table with layer = "dyn" and no format field, then
one table with all fields per target, then WVN7Dyn[zoneId] = DynTargets) with the
common fields id, kind, type, category, nameEn, nameDe, searchAliases, nativeMapId,
coordinateSpace, x, y, z, zStatus, availability, source, positionBasis and entry. It contains 242
targets of two kinds (the dyn_targets.lua of Moonglade and Mount Hyjal have none):

  kind "transit_facility" (102 targets), with a facilityKind:
    dungeon   20   An instance entrance (15 different dungeons). Role "entrance". The position is
                   where a player appears when leaving the instance (positionBasis
                   "teleport_exit_arrival").
    boat      15   A boat dock. Role "waiting".
    zeppelin  10   A zeppelin or skyboat dock. Role "waiting".
    lift      34   An elevator platform. Role "waiting" (the upper and the lower station are
                   separate targets).
    tram       8   The Deeprun Tram: the entrances in Stormwind and Ironforge ("entrance"), the two
                   exits inside the tunnel ("exit"), and the four waiting areas of the tram
                   ("waiting").
    portal    11   A portal. Six are entered by walking into them (for example the portals of the
                   Stormwind mage tower or the one at Rut'theran Village). Role "entrance".
                   Five belong to the project transitions of Zephras Isle (see below): the portal
                   to Rohashi Spires and its arrival, and the portal to Valanaar with one arrival
                   per faction (role "exit"; actionKind "portal", actionMode "click",
                   positionBasis "project_transition").
    wind_bridge 2  The Wind Bridge of Zephras Isle (see below): its waiting point (role "entrance",
                   actionKind "wind_bridge", actionMode "wait_for_signal") and its landing (role
                   "exit"). positionBasis "project_transition".
    dive       2   The dive transition at the Ruins of Blackfathom in Darkshore: "Dive here" (role
                   "entrance") and "Surface here" (role "exit"), with actionKind "dive" or
                   "surface" and positionBasis "project_dive_transition".
  kind "zone_transition" (140 targets). A crossing to a neighbouring zone (see below).

Common transit fields
  facilityKind, facilityRole   As above. role is "entrance", "exit" or "waiting".
  sourceKey, transportRouteId, transportStopId
                 Source identifiers of a vehicle or a stop. They stay stable between exports.
  haltAxis       The halt axis {x,y,z} of the vehicle in the source data; the waiting point was
                 derived from it.
  lift           Timing of an elevator: periodSeconds, dwellSeconds, stop, stops, ...
  interactionMode "manual_wait" for docks: the player waits at a waiting point (waitingRadius,
                 at most 2 yd) and boards the vehicle personally.
  activationMode "walk_into_trigger" for entrances and exits: the player walks into a trigger
                 volume. activationPreX/Y/Z is a point from which to walk in, activationCapture-
                 Radius is the tolerance, volume describes the trigger (sphere or oriented box).
  teleport       The destination of a walk-in trigger: {mapId, x, y, z, sameMap, ...}.
  teleports      Only on the entrance of the Valanaar portal: one destination per faction, each
                 with a "faction" field ("horde" or "alliance"); teleport then holds the first.
  actionKind, actionMode
                 The kind of the matching action in navdata.lua ("portal", "wind_bridge", "dive",
                 "surface"; section 6) and, where the player does not just walk in, how the
                 action is activated: "click" (interact with a portal object) or "wait_for_signal"
                 (wait at the waiting point of the Wind Bridge until its signal, no time limit).
                 Present on the dive points and the project transitions of Zephras Isle.
  faction        "alliance" or "horde" on the two arrival targets of the Valanaar portal (one per
                 faction); the other targets do not have it.
  approximate    true on the seven project transitions of Zephras Isle: their positions are
                 estimates. The demo addon announces such a position as approximate.
  decision       A note naming the decision behind a project transition of Zephras Isle.
  boardingHintStatus, boardingDirX, boardingDirY, boardingPreviewDistance
                 "certified" when a boarding orientation is known: (boardingDirX, boardingDirY) is a
                 unit vector in the world plane, and the point that lies boardingPreviewDistance
                 yards from the waiting point in that direction is the orientation point (the demo
                 addon uses it as a boarding hint). "unavailable" otherwise.
  destinationEn, destinationMapId   The place a vehicle goes to.
  positionBasis  How x, y, z was chosen (e.g. "halt_axis", "trigger_volume_centre",
                 "project_transition").
  waitpointProofId, approachProofId   Names of the proof records that tie this target to its
                 capture record in navdata.lua (see below).

How a dyn target is tied to the network
  A dyn target itself holds no waypoint ID. The physical connection is in the navdata of the zone: the
  facility is an ACTION waypoint, the capture record in the "captures" extra repeats the key values of
  the target (position, proof IDs, mode, radii), and, where the destination is part of the data,
  there is an action (the transit link) for portals, elevators, tram entrances, the dive points and
  the Wind Bridge. A consumer binds
  the target by position like any other target; the capture record is the certificate that the
  position is a genuine waiting point or trigger. The
  demo addon accepts a transit target as a certified interaction only when the capture record in the
  navdata equals the target (position to 0.1 yd and all listed keys).

Localised names (nameKey)
  Every dyn target except two carries a "nameKey" and usually a parameter, so that the consumer can
  speak the name in the player's language: the key picks a text template, and the place comes from
  the client:

    nameKey           Meaning
    dungeon_entrance  Dungeon entrance (parameter: the dungeon)
    portal            Portal (parameter: the destination)
    dock_ship         Boat dock (parameter: the destination)
    dock_zeppelin     Zeppelin or skyboat dock (parameter: the destination)
    elevator          Elevator
    tram_entrance     Tram entrance
    tram_dock         Tram waiting area (parameter: the direction)
    dive_entry        "Dive here"
    dive_exit         "Surface here"
    rohashi_portal, rohashi_arrival
                      Portal to Rohashi Spires, and the arrival there
    valanaar_portal   Portal to Valanaar
    valanaar_arrival_alliance, valanaar_arrival_horde
                      Alliance / Horde arrival in Valanaar Sanctuary
    wind_bridge_wait, wind_bridge_landing
                      Wind Bridge waiting point, and its landing
    zone_transition   Crossing to a neighbouring zone (parameter: the neighbour)

  nameAreaId (an AreaTable ID) or nameMapId (a map ID) gives the place; nameParam is the English name
  as a fallback. nameSuffix = {key, value} is added when two targets of a zone would be spoken alike:
  key is "lower" or "upper" for two floors, "z" with a height, or "number" with a running number
  (the shipped files use "lower", "upper" and "number").
  The demo addon has the templates in its Localization.lua. nameEn and nameDe hold ready-made texts.
  The ready-made names of zone_transition targets read "Übergang <zone>" in both languages (the
  German word for "transition"); build the spoken text from nameKey and nameParam instead.

Zone transitions (kind "zone_transition")
  Such a target is the recommended place at which a player walks from one zone into a neighbour: a
  position (x, y, z) at a crossing. It names the neighbour by nameParam and nameAreaId. Where the
  crossing is a seam (112 of the 140 targets), seamId is the ID of that seam in the index. The place
  is chosen where most routes between the two zones cross (positionBasis "seam_route_majority", 112
  targets); the others use the centre of the reachable gateways ("gateway_centre_of_reachable", 28
  targets). The extra fields (gateways, gatewayCount,
  reachableGateways, roadShare, seamPairs, seamRoutes, seamTied, chosenGateway, gatewayDistance)
  document how the place was chosen; a consumer does not need them.


9. ZONE TRANSITIONS IN GENERAL
------------------------------

Each zone is generated alone, and the connections to the zones that exist already are added as
seams. Pairs of zones that touch on foot have seams; a pair without a walkable crossing has none.
Burning Steppes and Riverglades, for example, meet at a cliff step without a footpath, and the way
between them leads through Redridge Mountains.

  On-foot seams     The large majority. A border is crossed by many seams, spread along its length,
                    so a route can cross wherever the border is walkable (for example 446 seams
                    between Durotar and The Barrens).
  Water seams       Where a border crosses a lake or the sea, the seam is a swimming connection
                    (water = true on the record). They are 1,533 of the 3,318 seams. A search
                    multiplies their cost by the swimming factor.
  Passages          Cities and tunnels are zones of their own, joined to their surroundings by
                    seams: Stormwind City with Elwynn Forest, Undercity with Tirisfal Glades,
                    Orgrimmar with Durotar and with The Barrens, Ironforge with Dun Morogh (2
                    seams). Felwood is joined to Moonglade and to Winterspring through the
                    Timbermaw Hold tunnel (2 seams each).
  Portals           A portal that leads to a neighbouring zone, such as the one between Rut'theran
                    Village in Teldrassil and Darnassus, is stored as an action with its destination
                    in the other zone (section 6), in addition to the seams between the zones.
  Deeprun Tram      The Deeprun Tram is an instance map of its own (native map 369) with a walkable
                    tunnel of about 2,560 yd. It has no seams. It is joined to Stormwind and to
                    Ironforge by walk-in actions: the trigger in Stormwind (2173) or in Ironforge
                    (2175) leads into the tunnel, and the triggers at the two ends of the tunnel
                    (2171 at the Stormwind end, 2166 at the Ironforge end) lead out again. The tram
                    itself has four ride actions ("board"), which are not part of a route in the
                    reference implementation, and four waiting areas, which are targets. A route
                    between Stormwind and Ironforge through the corridor therefore goes on foot
                    through the tunnel. A player who prefers the tram chooses its waiting area as
                    the destination.
  Dive transition   The Ruins of Blackfathom in Darkshore are joined to the coast by a pair of
                    actions ("dive" and "surface") between the points "Dive here" and "Surface here".
  Zephras Isle      Zephras Isle (native map 2991) has no seams and no walking connection to another
                    zone. Inside the zone, the ledge of the Rohashi Spires and the ritual platform
                    are joined to the main island by three transitions of the project (all with
                    approximate positions, see section 8): a portal that is clicked, from the
                    main island to the ledge (portal:rohashi_portal); the Wind Bridge from the
                    ledge to the ritual platform (wind_bridge:wind_bridge), where the player waits
                    at the waiting point until the bridge signal and then steps into the wind
                    current, without a time limit; and a clicked portal from the platform to
                    Valanaar Sanctuary on the main island, with one arrival per faction
                    (portal:valanaar_portal:alliance and :horde, each used only by the players of
                    that faction). All four actions lead from the zone into the same zone.
  Vehicles          Boats, zeppelins and skyboats are not part of routes. Their docks are targets
                    (dyn_targets.lua). Zephras Isle has two docks (zeppelin or skyboat connections
                    to Dalaran in Alterac Mountains and to Thunder Bluff). Flight paths do not
                    appear in the data.


10. USING THE DATA: LOAD, BIND, ROUTE, GUIDE
--------------------------------------------

The following is the procedure that the demo addon (NavNetwork.lua and its neighbours) follows. It
is a good model for other implementations.

 1. LOAD  Load all files at start-up (the demo lists them in its .toc). Keep the raw tables, and read
    the continent indexes into a table of zones and a table of seams. A logical catalogue is kept
    in its compact form (small arrays; about 7 MB of file for all zones). The demo checks the
    catalogues of the active continent when the continent becomes active and reads the compact
    rows as they are; it builds one table per target only for the targets it needs (the hits of
    a search, a single target by ID): for each row the class gives kind, categories, entry,
    names and availability, and the header gives the native map, the coordinate space and the
    default availability (section 7).

 2. LOCATE  Find the zone of the player. The demo asks the client for the player's current map and
    walks up the parent maps until it finds a UiMap ID that is listed in the "uiMaps" of a zone; an
    instance such as the Deeprun Tram is found by its native map ID. The world boxes turn a
    position on the world map into world coordinates (section 3) and select candidate zones for a
    world point. If the zone is not available, the consumer should say so.

 3. DECODE ON DEMAND  Decode a zone only when a route needs it: the zone of the start, the zone of
    the target, and every zone that the search enters through a seam. Decoding means the running
    sums of x, y and z (divided by 10), the lookups in the code dictionary, the link table from lo,
    lt and lf (computing the length of each link from the coordinates), the extras, and the cell
    tables (origins, bands, attachments). A zone is decoded once and then kept. The demo addon also
    decodes the zones that share a seam with the current zone in the background.

 4. BIND THE START  Find the cells above the player's position (x, y) whose height band contains the
    player's height with a tolerance of 3 yd. FINE cells (8 yd) are tried before OPEN cells (16 yd);
    a swimming player uses only WATER cells (32 yd), for which only the upper bound of the band
    counts (a point under the surface belongs to the water cell above it). If no cell contains the
    point, take the nearest cell within 3 yd. Among the attachments of the cell, prefer those whose
    field mask has the bit of the player's field set (nearest first), then those of the neighbouring
    fields, then all.
    An attachment is a waypoint or a point on a link; a point on a link becomes a temporary waypoint
    that is joined to both ends of the link. If the height is not known and several floors are
    stacked at the position, the choice is ambiguous; the demo addon then uses its running route,
    the last known height, or asks the player. The demo addon found that the client does not report
    a usable player height in the open world, so a consumer should be ready for this case.

 5. BIND THE TARGET  As described in section 7: the cell above (or nearest to) the target, then an
    attachment of the cell. Other attachments of the cell are tried in order when no route reaches
    the first.

 6. ROUTE  One A* search over the union of all decoded zones, from the start attachment to the
    target attachment. The edges are:
      * links, in their allowed direction; cost = (length + 15 yd) * a factor. The 15 yd are a
        hop cost: they count the waypoint the player has to steer to (partial links to seam
        points, to seams and to actions carry none). The reference implementation
        uses 1 for plain links, 1.5 for FINE links and 1.5 * (1 + p/100) for swimming links, where
        p is a comfort setting of the player (default 60). Road links get a reduced factor in a
        second pass (the hop cost is included in both passes), and the result of that pass is used
        when its geometric length is at most (1 + p/100) times that of the plain pass.
      * seams, as described in section 5, with their cost (times the swimming factor for water
        seams), in both directions;
      * actions (transit links), if the consumer allows them. The reference implementation allows
        every action except boarding a vehicle: walk-in portals and triggers, clicked portals, the
        dive and surface actions, the Wind Bridge and elevators. An action with a faction is used
        only for a player of that faction.
    A route is the list of waypoints that the search passes, with the temporary points at its ends.
    The demo addon skips a BRANCH waypoint that is merely walked through.

 7. GUIDE  For each waypoint the player should walk to, announce the direction and the distance.
    The waypoint counts as reached when the player is within its arrival radius (from the data,
    optionally raised by a setting), or has crossed the waypoint's gate line (a line perpendicular
    to the incoming link, twice the half width long), or, in OPEN terrain, has crossed the angle
    bisector at the waypoint and stands in the attachment cell of the next one. A player who leaves
    the route and enters the attachment cell of a later waypoint can continue there.

 8. NEW ZONES AND MAPS  A route that crosses a seam simply continues in the next zone. After a
    transit action to another map (the Deeprun Tram), the zones of the new map are decoded and
    routing continues there.


11. THE DEMO ADDON
------------------

DemoAddon/WowVisionNavigation is the reference implementation of the procedure above. It is a
working addon with these parts:

  NavNetwork.lua        Decoder, graph over the zones, seams, attachment cells, A* search.
  Guidance.lua          Arrival at a waypoint, gate line, angle bisector, height and re-entry.
  TargetCatalog.lua     The catalogues of all zones of the active continent; it validates the compact
                        logical catalogues (section 7), turns rows into one table per target on
                        demand (functions Materialize and ExpandCatalog), scans the compact rows for
                        the target search and refuses any other format of a logical catalogue.
  TargetResolver.lua    Binds targets to attachment cells (exact, approximate, ambiguous).
  TargetProviders.lua, TargetSelection.lua
                        Live targets (corpse, group member) and the choice of a target.
  ActionNavigation.lua, DockNavigation.lua, TransitActivation.lua
                        Transit: actions (walk-in, clicked portals, the Wind Bridge, dive points),
                        docks and walk-in triggers.
  Localization.lua      All player-facing texts, in German and English.
  WowVisionNavigation.lua
                        The game integration: zone detection, the /vn commands, events, sounds.
  Sounds/               Audio cues for direction and arrival.

According to its .toc, the addon reads game state only and never controls the character.

Installation, as given in DemoAddon/readme.txt:
  * Copy the addon folder (WowVisionNavigation) into the AddOns folder of the game installation.
  * Copy the navigation data you want to test into its "maps" folder, keeping the layout of this
    repository: Forever/<Continent> becomes WowVisionNavigation/maps/<Continent>. The .toc lists
    every file under maps/.
  * Optional partner addons: WowVision (accessible target list window and turning with the I key;
    without it the addon uses its own fallback list window and turns the character with the
    camera) and Questie (quest objectives for /vn q when the game's own quest data has none).
    (The .toc declares Questie as an optional dependency.)

The .toc declares interface 16001 (WoW Forever 1.60.1) and also 50504 (MoP Classic 5.5.4); the data
of this repository describes WoW Forever only.


12. QUALITY OF THE DATA
-----------------------

Every zone was checked when it was generated. In general terms:

  * Coverage. For each zone, positions on walkable ground were sampled and tested for whether they
    can be attached to the network with a route. In every zone at least about 95 percent of the
    sampled positions can; the values lie between 95.6 percent (Orgrimmar) and 99.4 percent
    (Mulgore), depending on the zone.
  * Target reachability. In all 51 zones at least 95 percent of the target groups are
    reachable through the network (5 zones reach 100 percent, 21 zones 99 percent or more). The
    Ruins of Blackfathom in
    Darkshore are reached by a dive transition point that announces "Dive here" at the entrance,
    and the targets on the ledge of the Rohashi Spires and on the ritual platform of Zephras Isle
    are reached through the project transitions (section 9).
    Seven groups of Shatterspear Vale remain unreachable (an isolated part of the water network).
    The tightest zones are Swamp of Sorrows (95.1 percent), Zephras Isle (95.2 percent) and Burning
    Steppes (95.9 percent). Targets that cannot be reached are not in the catalogues.
    A target group is all the spawns of one creature or object entry in a zone (type and entry,
    for example all the boars of a zone); one reachable member is enough. The percentage of a zone
    is the number of reachable groups divided by the number of counted groups. Counted are the
    groups of all target rows of the zone before the unreachable ones were removed; a group is
    reachable when at least one of its spawns attaches to the main connected part of the zone's
    network (a seam end towards a neighbour counts as part of it). Zone crossings are not groups,
    and in the Deeprun Tram the groups whose spawns lie outside the mapped tunnel (5) are not
    counted. The catalogues hold only the reachable spawns, so the percentage cannot be recomputed
    from the shipped files alone.
  * Target sources. The catalogues are built from the QuestieDB of WoW Forever (version 1.0.9)
    and Thottbot4ever. An entry that the old base data does not know counts only when it is
    confirmed to exist in WoW Forever, by the Thottbot4ever name list or by Wowhead Forever
    (the second confirmation source). German names come from Wowhead.
  * Link checks. Every link and every attachment was verified offline on the walkable geometry of
    the game.
  * Seams. All seam ends point to existing waypoints or links, and every GATEWAY end lists its seam
    ID (section 5).
  * Size. No navigation file of a zone exceeds 3 MB (the largest is about 2.6 MB).

These are measurements taken when the data was built. They are not a guarantee for every position
in the game world.


13. VERSIONING AND UPDATES
--------------------------

Format version
  The format of navdata.lua is "Schema 7": version=7 in every file header. The target catalogues
  carry schema=1; a logical catalogue also carries format=2, the compact form of section 7 (the
  dyn catalogue has no format field). The version number is the compatibility marker: an
  implementation that expects version 7 should refuse files with another number, and one that
  reads format 2 should refuse a format it does not know. This ReadMe describes version 7.

How the data is made
  The files are generated zone by zone from the game's terrain, buildings and tables, and from
  public databases for the targets. The faction outposts (W.factions) are added to a finished
  navdata.lua in a separate step; it changes nothing else in the file. They are not meant to be
  edited by hand. A manual change would
  break more than it fixes: the files refer to each other by waypoint numbers, link numbers and
  seam IDs, and the fingerprint in the index must match the one in the zone file.

What a regeneration changes
  * A new export of a zone renumbers its waypoints, links and cells, and gives it a new fingerprint.
    The index and the zone files of the same export belong together; do not mix them with files of
    another export.
  * The seam records of the pairs that include the zone are replaced as well.
  * The target catalogues hold no waypoint numbers, so they can be refreshed without touching
    navdata.lua.

When you update your copy of the data, replace the whole Forever folder, or at least the index of the
continent together with the files of every zone that changed.


14. KNOWN LIMITATIONS
---------------------

  * Routes are on foot only. Boats, zeppelins, skyboats, the tram and flight paths are not part of
    routes. Their docks are targets.
  * Faction marking is data only. 41 of the 51 zones carry the marking W.factions (section 6); the
    other 10 zones are neutral. The marking forbids nothing: a consumer that ignores it may lead a
    player through outposts and capital cities of the opposing faction.
  * No avoidance of dangerous areas. Targets and waypoints carry no information about enemy
    creatures or their levels (the "l" field of a target is a list of floor heights).
  * Water is two-dimensional. A body of water has one surface, and there are no diving routes into
    flooded caves. The one exception is the dive transition at the Ruins of Blackfathom in
    Darkshore: a pair of points that work like a portal, "Dive here" at the entrance.
  * No dungeon or raid interiors. The Deeprun Tram is the only instance map. Dungeon entrances are
    included only where a source gave their position; entrances of some dungeons, in particular of
    dungeons that are new in WoW Forever, are missing.
  * The Ruins of Gilneas are not included: the client does not ship terrain or a map for them.
  * Some places cannot be reached: Dalaran in Alterac Mountains (a magical barrier).
  * Estimated transitions. The positions of the portals and the Wind Bridge of Zephras Isle
    (section 9) are estimates (approximate = true). The player has to act at them: click the
    portal, or wait at the Wind Bridge for its signal.
  * Some targets are only approximate. A target whose position lies outside every attachment cell
    is attached to the nearest cell (up to 30 yd away in the demo addon) and reported as
    approximate. A target without a source height over several floors needs a floor choice.
  * Creatures from the Forever database usually carry no role (category npc_or_mob), and their
    German name falls back to the English one when none is known. Creature positions are spawn
    points; creatures move.
  * The player height. A consumer cannot rely on the game to report the player's height in the open
    world, so stacked floors (stairs, towers, multi-storey buildings) can be ambiguous.
  * Names of zone transitions. The ready-made nameEn and nameDe of zone_transition targets read
    "Übergang <zone>" in both languages (section 8).
